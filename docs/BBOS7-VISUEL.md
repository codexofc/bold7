# Visual reference: BlackBerry OS 7.1 on the Bold 9900

This document is the source for everything Bold7 shows or plays, element by element: value, source, reliability, and how it is adapted to the Q25's square screen. It is built from the official 9900 simulator, the 9900's 7.1 firmware and period captures.

## Sources and reliability

Each statement carries a grade:

- **A**: the 9900's 7.1 firmware (images, `.pme` layouts, fonts, sounds, values of the binary `theme.xml`);
- **B**: official RIM documentation (7.1 user guide, simulator help);
- **C**: native capture from a real device or from the official simulator, measured to the pixel;
- **D**: deduction, in particular any layout recalculated for the square screen;
- **by eye**: looked at on a capture without measurement or source, to be confirmed before drawing anything.

A and B are the F and M of the grades in README.md and CONTRIBUTING.md. The captures under `assets-private/reference-bbos7/` are the maintainer's reference set and are not published (they show BlackBerry's interface); their identifiers (sim-NN, gsm NNN, ps NNNNN, vid-NN) name each capture so that a value can be traced, and anyone can take the same captures on the official simulator.

The 9900 has a 640x480 screen at 287 ppi, the Q25 a 720x720 screen at 295 ppi: a 9900 pixel and a Q25 pixel are almost the same size (2.7 % apart). **All measurements are in 9900 pixels and carry over unchanged to the Q25.** The simulator's colours are quantised to RGB565 (hence `#313031` instead of `#303030`): the value read is given, and the theme image, when there is one, is authoritative.

Caveats on the sources. The firmware at hand carries the `Wind` suffix (a carrier) and contains a China Unicom boot video: anything that could come from a carrier is flagged. The simulator is generic (RIM), but its wallpaper, its network ("Simulated Network", EDGE) and its installed apps are not necessarily those of a factory 9900. The GSMArena captures are on 7.0; they are only used for cross-checking.

### Piloting the simulator

To take a reference capture next to a Q25 capture, the simulator is driven without a mouse, from the simulator folder, with `WINEPREFIX=$HOME/.wine-bold7-simulateur WINEDEBUG=-all`:

- launch at 100 %, screen only (a 640x480 window that fits on the Mac screen, no blocking dialog): the original command line followed by `/show-plastics=false /zoom=1 /automate /automate-timeout=1`. Without `/zoom=1`, the simulator shrinks to fit the screen and the native capture comes out at 418x313;
- commands: one per line on the standard input of `wine fledgecontroller.exe /session=9900`, the command list coming from the binary's `…Command` classes and from `fledge-user.chm`: `KeyPress(K)` then `KeyRelease(K)`, `TrackballRoll(dx,dy)` (negative dy is down), `TrackballPress` then `TrackballRelease` (click; held 2 s, short menu), `StringInjection("text")`, `Pause(s)`, `RemoteCallCreation("5551234")`, `RemoteCallDisconnection("5551234")`, `SaveLcdSnapshot(C:\\cap\\name.png)` (path with doubled backslashes; the file arrives one or two seconds late);
- keys (`KeyT` enumeration in `enums.txt` and `9900.conf` inside `fledge.dat`): Menu = `FRONT_CONVENIENCE`, Back = `ESCAPE`, Send = `SEND`, End = `END`, lock = `LOCK`, volume = `VOLUME_UP` / `VOLUME_DOWN`, letters `A` to `Z`, `ENTER`, `SPACE`, `_0` to `_9`, Backspace = `BACKSPACE` (`DEL` is rejected). A held letter (a pause between `KeyPress` and `KeyRelease`) types its capital, as on the real keyboard: for a password, `KeyPress` and `KeyRelease` with no pause, and a pause between two letters.
- locking with the password (`bold`): type L, O, C, K on the home screen and open **Password Lock** (sim-90); the `LOCK` key only locks the keyboard. With the screen off, `ESCAPE` wakes it on "Device is locked"; **`U`** picks Unlock (the trackpad click and `ENTER` do nothing there), then the letters one by one (`StringInjection` types nothing here) and `ENTER`. Always finish with the right password: past the allowed number of attempts, the simulator wipes everything.

**Charging bolt** (A: `homescreen-banner.pme`, `banner.pme`, `lockscreen.pme`, the same in all three; B: guide 7.1 p. 20, "Battery is charging"; C: sim-470, sim-471, sim-415). While the battery charges, a bolt animates over it, in the three banners (home, lock screen, an app's bar):

- **Images**: `lightning_bolt_01` to `_05` (18x29, A), the same orange bolt from faint to opaque; `_00` is a 1x1 transparent image (none).
- **Place**: the pme puts the group `lightningBoltbatteryAnimation` at (20, 0), 18x29, for the battery frame at (3, 2) (its coordinates read as big-endian integers next to each element's name, checked against the frames already measured: battery (3, 2), alarm (63, 4), time box 208 wide); i.e. (+17, -2) from the frame, the bolt overflowing it by 2 px above and below. sim-470 confirms it to the pixel (bolt composited at (20, 0) over a capture without it: the smallest difference). On the one-line bar of an app (Calendar, sim-471) the battery frame is at (428, 2) and the bolt at (444, 2): (+16, 0) (C).
- **Rhythm**: a discrete animation (`LightningbatteryChargingAnimation`) of the frames `l00` to `l11`, which show images 0 1 2 3 4 5 4 3 2 1 0 0 (each `l` refers to its image by index in the pme's string table), at `keyTimes` 0, .05, .1, .15, .2, .3, .75, .8, .85, .9, .95, 1 (sixteen-bit fractions, the first and last implied), `dur` 1800 ms (`07 08`), repeated, from `batteryStatus_isCharging` to `batteryStatus_isNotCharging` (A). So: fade in over 0.54 s (90 ms per image, the fourth 180 ms), the full bolt for 0.81 s, fade out over 0.36 s, nothing for 90 ms. The simulator gives the same (C): 200 timed frames of its window, period fitted at 1.80 s, time per image 10 / 9 / 9 / 12 / 12 / 48 % against 10 / 10 / 10 / 10 / 15 / 45 % expected.
- **Under the bolt** the battery keeps its own row (fill by level, no plug): sim-470 at 50 %, the guide's icon (B). The plug row is the charger connected once charging has stopped, the battery full (sim-415, C).
- **When** (D for the Android source): Android's status `CHARGING` with a level under 100 %. The Q25 stays `Charging` at 100 % while its charger chip says `Full`: at 100 % Bold7 shows the full battery with the plug, no bolt. Android's level is read, not `/sys/class/power_supply/upm6922-charger/status`. Stopped while the screen is off or the banner hidden (no frame, no wake-up), from where it was when it resumes.

On the Q25: `ChargingBolt` (`core/design`, its rule and keyTimes), drawn by the launcher's banner, the lock screen's face and, in an app's bar, `BatteryImage` under LineageOS 24 or `BatteryBolt` in Android's `BatteryMeterView` under BenOS (which reads the charging status from the sticky battery broadcast), each through `BoltTicker` (`core/ui`): one redraw per change of image, on the view's own thread.

**Trackpad focus** (C, sim-22, sim-25, sim-28): the trackpad moves from the drawer to the indicator row, then to the banner. Three targets: **the whole banner** (blue background over 640x93; a click opens Manage Connections, § 6), **the central notifications area** (blue strip from x ≈ 47 to 593; a click opens the notification list, § 5), **the speaker** and **the magnifier** (a blue square each). The missed call icon gets a red cross when its area has focus (C, sim-25).

`BannerTime.cbtf` and the other `.cbtf` files are not TTFs. Read by `tools/cbtf.py` (A): a container of 7 sections (name, glyph count, font metrics, offset of each glyph, character ranges with a bit mask, glyphs, a palette of 27 whites with varying alpha); each glyph carries 6 bytes of metrics then its pixels. BannerTime's metrics can be read: digits with an advance of 27 to 30 px ("0" 29, "1" 17), 40 px high (flat) to 42 px (round, 1 px below the baseline), ":" with a 9 px advance and 29 high, AM and PM in 12 px capitals; it also contains `.`, `/`, the Latin letters and four Greek letters (μ.μ. and π.μ.). **The pixels cannot be read yet**: a bit stream (most significant bit first) whose coding is not identified; transparent areas are repeated `011`, opaque areas repeated 8-bit patterns (`00001101`, `00000101`, at every rotation); these are neither raw palette indices (5 bits) nor a simple run-length coding of those indices, and the decoder is native OS code, absent from the theme. Fallback: the banner time in BBAlpha Sans Condensed 54 px ("1" of 39 px, "0" of 41 when rendered, by eye), with BannerTime's advances.

### Screen transitions

**Source** (A): `theme.xml` of the `_b` theme (the same in the 9900 firmware and in the simulator; `_bs1` has none), screen transitions section, decoded by hand from its binary format (see below, no tool in the repository reads it yet). Each rule names a source screen, a destination screen or both, an event, a transition kind and its attributes. The kind and attributes follow `TransitionContext` from RIM's Java API: fade, slide (with direction), wipe, zoom (with scale in percent), none; direction "in" or "out". The rules that concern Bold7:

| Screen | Event | Transition |
|---|---|---|
| `homescreen`, when leaving (to any screen) | 4 | zoom, in, scale 75 |
| `homescreen`, when arriving (from any screen) | 4 | zoom, out, scale 75 |
| `application-switcher`, when arriving | 2 | zoom, in, scale 110 |
| `application-switcher`, when leaving | 3 | zoom, out, scale 110 |
| `menu`, `tooltip` | 2, 3 | none |
| `statusbar` (probably the banner's connections panel) | 2, 3, 4 | zoom, scale 75 |
| `todayhomescreen` (home screen of the Today theme, not the default 9900 one) | 4 | a kind not decoded (`0x68`); to the switcher: zoom 110, back 125 |

**Format** (for re-reading): `theme.xml` is zlib-compressed; once decompressed, each rule starts with the byte `0x34` followed by its length, then the attributes `K` (source screen), `L` (destination screen), `M` (duration, as text), `N` (event, `0x32` to `0x34`), then an element whose first byte is the kind (`0x35` fade, `0x36` slide, `0x38` zoom, `0x39` none; `0x65` and `0x68` not decoded) followed by its attributes: `O` the direction (`0x35` in, `0x36` out), `0x32` the direction of a slide, `0x81 0x10` the scale of a zoom (16-bit integer, little-endian). The home screen rule is found by searching for the byte `0x0a` followed by `homescreen`. This reading of the bytes is an inference (D), consistent across the 208 rules (forward and back pairs always invert, the names `calendar-fade-in`, `-zoom-in`, `-right-to-left` land on fade, zoom and slide).

No duration on the zooms (the slides carry one, 250 ms, and two fades 150 ms); no rule for dialogs nor between apps.

**Reading** (D, to be confirmed by video): event 2 is pushing a screen, 3 popping it, 4 both; "in" animates the arriving screen, "out" the departing one. The zoom changes scale **and** opacity: an "out, 110" rule (the departing screen grows to 110 %) would not be visible without a fade, since the departing screen stays on top. Hence:

- **opening an app from the home screen**: the app grows from 75 to 100 % as it appears, centred;
- **returning to the home screen** (Escape, End, home key): the app shrinks from 100 to 75 % as it fades out, the home screen below;
- **switcher**: the theme also has `application-switcher-animation-on = false` (§ 4, A), which contradicts the zoom rule; Bold7 keeps the switcher without animation (the rule: a source that says "no animation" wins), the video will decide;
- **an app opened from the switcher**: no rule between the switcher and an app: no animation.

**On the Q25 (720x720).** Zoom centred on the app window, 250 ms (A: `TransitionContext`'s default duration, § 36), an ease-out (A: `TransitionContext`'s default pacing of a zoom, § 36) drawn as `EASINGCURVE_QUADRATIC_OUT` (D: which ease-out the firmware computes is not read), opacity linear in the curved time (D). Code: `Transitions` in `apps/launcher/.../quickstep/Zoom.kt`, the only place where the shape is set.

**Video (§ 36, C)**: the simulator (graphics acceleration Off, its default setting) opens and closes an app **with a cut**, no zoom or fade (12 changes out of 12); the switcher appears at once and closes with a **fade of 120 to 160 ms with no change of scale**. This contradicts the reading above; the table and the video are to be reconciled in § 36.

**7.1 UI Guidelines, p. 40, "Screen transitions" (B)**: fade ("Set the duration of the fade to 150 milliseconds") when the content is the same seen differently; slide between items of the same level (the next comes in from the right, the previous from the left); zoom between levels (in from a parent to its child, out from the child to the parent). For creating, sending or saving an item: "When users open the application from the Home screen, zoom in from the center of the screen. The zoom transition is the most common type of transition between screens. Set the scale of the zoom to 110 percent." **Reading kept**: the 110 % is the general advice to apps, the table's 75 % is the home screen rule, more specific; Bold7 keeps the app growing from 75 to 100 %, centred, 250 ms (A, `TransitionContext`'s default, § 36).

## 36. Transitions filmed on the simulator

**Source** (C): nine videos of the 9900 simulator on 7.1 (`assets-private/reference-bbos7/videos/vid-01` to `vid-09`, `SOURCES.md`), frames and durations read frame by frame, and the captures sim-318 to sim-339. The window is filmed alone (`screencapture -v -l`), then cropped to the 640x480 screen. **Actual frame rate of the file**: one frame at each change of the screen, with 17 ms gaps within a burst (60 frames per second at most) and no frame at rest (92 frames for 3 s of an almost still screen, 115 for 42 s of the switcher take). **The simulator itself only redraws its screen about every 42 ms (24 Hz)**: the measured gaps between two changing frames are 41, 42, 42, 34 ms (switcher), 42, 41, 50 ms (banner). Any duration therefore reads to within 42 ms, and a 125 ms fade has only three or four frames.

**Opacity measurement**: for each frame of the gesture, the share of the destination image in the measured frame (projection onto the segment between the frame before and the frame after, exact for a fade between two images). The window's scale is read by comparing the position of elements from one frame to the next: **none of the filmed gestures changes scale** (the switcher icons stay in the same place and at the same size throughout the fade; the Text Messages thread and the banner panel keep their 640 width). The centre of a zoom is therefore irrelevant.

### Against the theme's table

The theme's transition table (§ 24: zoom in of 75 % when leaving the home screen, zoom out of 75 % when returning to it, zoom of 110 % for the switcher) **is not played by the simulator as it runs**: opening and returning from an app are hard cuts (12 changes out of 12, without a single frame at an intermediate opacity), and the switcher opens at once and closes with a 120 to 160 ms fade without any change of scale. Whether the real device does the same cannot be stated. **The simulator's View > Graphics Acceleration menu is set to Off** (the default setting, with Low, Medium, High and Max Quality to choose from) and the simulator has no transition setting; with High Quality the screen does redraw (SaveLcdSnapshot renders the home screen) but the window stays a grey-black gradient, which both `screencapture -l` and `ffmpeg -f avfoundation` film without the content, and each capture takes 4.5 s to come out; the change restarts the simulated device and the simulator then stayed unresponsive. **The accelerated path could therefore not be filmed**, and it is the path of a real 9900, which may very well play the table's zooms. Open question: keep the measured cuts, or keep the table's zoom (D) until there is a video of a real device (or a way to film the accelerated path).

### The home screen zoom in the firmware: TransitionContext's defaults (A)

**Source** (A): the 9900 OS Loader (`CodFiles/net_rim_cldc.cod`, a zip of sibling modules), modules `net_rim_cldc-34.cod` and `net_rim_cldc-17.cod`, unpacked with the `unpack` of `tools/extract-bbos7.py` into a scratch directory (nothing written under `assets-private/`) and read by hand: strings pushed by `28 <ptr>` resolved in the data section, `tableswitch` (`2f`, u16 count, i32 low, then the default and one 16-bit offset per case, each relative to its own field), `sipush` (`25` and 16 bits), `iconst_0` (`23`), `iconst_1` (`2c`), virtual calls by slot (`de <slot>`: `0c` the type, `19` has an attribute, `21` its int value, `1d` its object value, the same slots in both modules). Offsets below are from the start of the module's code section. The public constants (API reference 7.1, M: `TRANSITION_FADE` 1, `SLIDE` 2, `WIPE` 3, `ZOOM` 4, `NONE` 5; `ATTR_DURATION` 1, `KIND` 2, `DIRECTION` 3, `STYLE` 4, `SCALE` 5) name the cases; the firmware adds a sixth attribute, the pacing (6).

| What | Where | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| `toString` of `TransitionContext` | `net_rim_cldc-17`, 0xb080 to 0xb290 | a switch on the type (`fade`, `slide`, `wipe`, `zoom`, `none` in that order, low 0), then each attribute printed or `default` when unset: `kind` (2), `direction` (3), `style` (4), `scale` (5, `%`), `duration` (1, `ms`), `pacing` (6: `ease-in`, `ease-out`, `ease-in-ease-out`, `linear` compared by identity to four static objects, else `other`) |
| Validation of a value | `net_rim_cldc-34`, switch at 0x018e | duration clamped to 0 to 1000 (`23 39 25 e8 03`), kind, direction, style kept in their range else 1, scale clamped to 0 to 200 (`25 c8 00`), anything else `invalid attribute` |
| **Default of an unset attribute** | `net_rim_cldc-34`, switch at 0x01f9 | **duration `sipush 250`** (0x020c), kind 1 (`KIND_IN`), direction 1 (`LEFT`), style 1 (`PUSH`), scale 0; the same values as the API reference's table (M: « 250 » for fade, slide, wipe and zoom) |
| **Pacing** | `net_rim_cldc-34`, 0x0233 to 0x027d | the pacing attribute if set (else `pacing function expected`); unset, a switch on the type (0x0261, low 1, the same coding as the `toString` switch, so slide, wipe, zoom) returns the static compared with `ease-out` in `toString` (0x0270), and the default case (fade, none) the one compared with `linear` (0x0277): **a zoom eases out, a fade is linear** |

The two `homescreen` rules of the theme (§ 24) set the kind (in leaving, out arriving) and the scale (75) but no duration (`M`) and no pacing: the zoom of the home screen is therefore **250 ms, ease-out** (A). The slides of the theme that carry `M 250` give the same figure.

Cross-check with the videos (C, shape only): the switcher leaving, a zoom of the theme without duration, is filmed as a fade that slows at the end (0.29; 0.72; 0.90; 0.99; 1), the ease-out shape; an app chosen in the switcher, which no rule names, as a linear fade (0.28; 0.53; 0.81; 1), the shape of a fade. The simulator without graphics acceleration plays them shorter (120 to 190 ms) than 250 ms and drops the scale.

**Still D**:
- which ease-out: the four pacing objects are statics of a class in another module (`07 3f ff 3d` is an import), whose curve was not followed; Bold7 draws `EASINGCURVE_QUADRATIC_OUT`, the theme's ease-out for a change of view;
- the fade: `TransitionContext` has no opacity attribute (type, kind, direction, style, scale, duration, pacing) and the API reference describes the zoom by its scale only; whether the 9900 fades the zooming screen, and in which order, is in the native compositor (`PROF: Composite: Transition`), not in the Java modules. Bold7 keeps the opacity linear in the curved time;
- the simulator's cut: nothing in the firmware turns the home screen rules off (only `application-switcher-animation-on = false` exists, for the switcher); the cut of the videos is the path without graphics acceleration, and no source shows a real 9900 cutting. The accelerated path was not filmed again (the attempts of § 36 above: the window filmed without its content, 4.5 s per snapshot).

The other help modules of the loader (`net_rim_bb_feeds_help_*`, `net_rim_bb_qm_*_help_*`, `net_rim_bb_plans_webkit_help_*`) belong to apps Bold7 does not have.

**The YK format** (A). Every `.ykz` is a run of blocks `84 00 <u16 big-endian length> <data>` closed by `83 00 00 00`; a page is one block whose data starts with `1f 6f 0e 14` (the same for every page of a module), `sidedata.data` a run of 1,020-byte blocks whose data starts with `00 3c 00 21`. The data is neither deflate (a raw inflate fails on every offset) nor LZMA. It is **Yang-Kieffer grammar-based compression**, RIM's `net.rim.device.internal.compress` (`YKDecode`, `YKInputStream`, `YKMixedInputStream`, `YKUtilities.ykDecode(data, cid, …)`, `YKSideDataProvider`, in `net_rim_cldc.cod` and `net_rim_os.cod`, read with coddec): `YKDecode.yk_decode0`, `yk_load_side_data0` and `isSupported` are **native**. The native code is C++ (`yk.cpp`, `yk_decode.cpp`, `yk_decode.inl`, `yk_grammar.cpp`, `yk_occ.cpp`, `yk_occheap.cpp`, `yk_reocc_coders.cpp`, `ykm_decode.cpp`, `../common/RangeCoder.cpp`, `../common/static_heap.cpp`): a grammar transform rebuilt by the decoder as it reads, with context models, reoccurrence coders and a range coder; its assertions name `vars`, `s0`, `groupings` (`CMAP_SIZE`), `parse_phrase`, `ESCAPE_SYMBOL`, `reoccurrence_start_off`, `use_static_coding`. It is in the simulator's `Jvm.dll` (x86, about 60,000 instructions for the whole YK library, encoder included, between `0x1044e165` and `0x1047ba90`) and, on the 9900, in the ARM OS image. The same format carries `net_rim_bb_email_compression.cod` (`compressionsidedata/static_html_id1.ykz`…) and BIS's `x-rim` HTTP encodings `yk` and `yk-mixed`. No public description of RIM's YK format was found by a web search; the published YK papers and RIM's patents (US8458365, "Synchronization of side information caches") describe the method, not RIM's bit stream.

**Reading it.** Bold7 now has its own YK decoder (`core/firmware`, `Yk.kt`; the format in `docs/YK.md`), written from the behaviour of the simulator's native decoder, which it matches byte for byte on all 1,173 pages of the OS Loader's `en_GB` help. The pages are plain XHTML for a WebKit browser field: `index.html` is the contents, a "Quick Help" group then "User Guide", each link a row with a `theme://cat_*` icon of the theme (60 px); a topic page has its title (`h1.topictitle1`), its text in `.content`, its links in `.ullinks` rows and a "Related information" footer back to Contents. Their only RIM tag is `<RIM type="supports" include|exclude="wifiText">` (268 and 258 of them): the 9900 has Wi-Fi, so the `include` side is shown. 47 links go to the web (`http`) and 11 start an app (`start://net_rim_bb_messaging_app`, `start://net_rim_bb_toolkit?…`); Bold7's Help refuses both. The style sheet draws the focus as `:hover`, the blue gradient `#04B9F7` to `#0B88ED` with white text (A). The topic names the apps pass (`messages_index`…) are mapped in the Help module's code, which Bold7 does not read: each Bold7 app opens the category of the contents named after it (D). The simulator's help (7.1.0.318, 1,327 files, 68,610 bytes of side data) is another edition whose dictionary leaves the grammar open, page after page (`docs/YK.md`, section 7): not read.

**Timing** (C, two sessions): every flash is lit **170 ms** (168 to 172), one every **3.37 s** (3.36 to 3.39), the same for the green of the coverage and the red of a ringing call. The "every 2.2 s" noted in `SOURCES.md` for a call is not what this session measured.

**One colour at a time** (C): while a call rang, the green of the coverage stopped and red took its place; green came back at the end of the call. Which wins between red, amber and blue is not seen (D in Bold7: red, then amber, then green).

**What the log does not show** (C, negative): a missed call, its dialog on screen or closed, or the screen locked by the Lock key, gave **no red flash** in 70 s; only green. The red of a new message could not be seen (the simulator receives no SMS). So the red after a call ends is not sourced; Bold7 flashes red for what is new in the Hub (the splat of Messages), a missed call included (D).

**Charging, switched on**: nothing (M). The Q25 does the same: Lineage's battery light is dark while charging (`dumpsys lights`, light 3 at 0), and the kernel's charging triggers are not set (not a defect once sourced).

**On the Q25**. The LED is three GPIOs (`leds-gpio`, `/sys/class/leds/{red,green,blue}`), on or off: amber is red and green together (D); the 9900's `0x3f` intensity has no equivalent. `bold7-led` (`system/module/led/`) flashes the colour the launcher writes to `bold7_led` (`apps/launcher/.../led/Led.kt`), 170 ms every 3.37 s, all three files written together. Bold7 does red (new in the Hub when Messages and Email's **LED:** is checked, and a ringing call; not under All Alerts Off, no message under Phone Calls Only, D), amber (Android's battery low, unplugged), green (Screen Display's **LED Coverage Indicator**, added to Bold7's Screen Display under Automatically Dim Backlight, clear by default). Not done: blue (default of its setting not sourced, and Bluetooth Connections is Android's page), the reminder's red, the per-profile LED (one LED setting for all profiles, D), Disable LED of the bedside mode (Bold7's Clock has no bedside mode).

**Asleep** (measured, kernel 5.10): the kernel's `timer` trigger works (170/2030 ms gave a 2.30 s period), but two of them (red and green for amber) drift 60 to 140 ms apart within a minute, hence one process for the three. The Q25's device tree has no `retain-state-suspended` for the LEDs: leds-gpio turns them **off while the kernel is suspended** and lights them back at the wake-up, whatever wrote them (the HAL's fixed colour as much as a flash). So the LED flashes while the phone is awake, and is dark during deep sleep: the 9900 flashed in a pocket, the Q25 flashes at each of its wake-ups. Keeping it awake to flash would be a periodic wake-up (refused by the project's rules).
